# Manuscript Section Playbook

Adapt headings to the target journal's house format.

## 1. Macro-structures by paper type

- **Empirical, physical, or scenario analysis:** `Abstract` -> `1. Introduction` -> `2. Data and Methods` (open with end-to-end model chain + `Figure 1` schematic, then data, definitions, and experimental setup) -> `3. Results` (by property, scale, season, and spatial co-occurrence) -> `4. Discussion` -> `5. Limitations` (dedicated or `Limitations and Discussion`) -> `6. Conclusions` -> `Data and Code Availability`.
- **Methodological or statistical framework:** Separate the general formulation from the specific case study: `Abstract` -> `1. Introduction` -> `2. Proposed Framework` (general math, likelihood, priors/estimation, numbered simulation/inference algorithm) -> `3. Application` (study region, data filtering, covariates, distributional/copula adequacy tests, implementation, baseline suite) -> `4. Results` -> `5. Discussion and Conclusions` -> `Data and Code Availability`.
- **Data descriptor:** `Abstract` -> `Background & Summary` (system value, dataset gap, coverage/scenarios, `Figure 1` pipeline) -> `Methods` (forcing, models, calibration metric/budget justification, operational approximations) -> `Data Records` (archive link, file naming, column-by-column schema with units and join keys) -> `Technical Validation` (calibration/validation split, cross-validation, station verification, multi-model benchmark comparison) -> `Usage Notes` -> `Code Availability`.

## 2. Section-by-section moves

- **Title & Highlights:** Use a specific noun phrase naming method/phenomenon, scale, and domain (no puns or hype). Keep Key Points to 3 parallel single-sentence claims (~12 to 20 words): contribution, primary quantitative result, and spatial/seasonal/operational takeaway.
- **Abstract (one paragraph, ~150 to 250 words):** (1) 1-2 sentences on system context and operational stakes; (2) 1-2 sentences on what prior datasets/models miss; (3) 1-2 sentences on the contribution (`In this study, we present/propose/examine ...`) with spatial unit and period; (4) 2-4 sentences of concrete quantitative/regional/seasonal results; (5) 1 sentence on planning or design consequences. For a _Plain Language Summary_, keep the same 5 moves while replacing jargon with plain operational phrases.
- **Introduction (4 to 7 paragraphs):**
  1. _Operational stakes:_ Connect the physical/statistical phenomenon directly to infrastructure design, risk, or operations.
  2. _Thematic literature synthesis (middle paragraphs):_ Group studies by scale, mechanism, or model class — never a chronological list. Credit studies (`Author et al. (Year) analyze ... finding ...`), then pivot (`However,`, `Despite this body of work,`) to expose structural trade-offs (single-site vs. network dependence, atmospheric-only vs. system impact, $O(n^3)$ scaling or low-rank artifacts, stationarity vs. climate/infrastructure change).
  3. _Contribution & roadmap:_ Pivot (`To address this,`, `Here we develop ...`), justify why the spatial unit and temporal resolution match the real decision boundary, optionally list 2 to 3 numbered research questions/properties, and end with a 2 to 4 sentence section roadmap.
- **Data and Methods:** Start multi-model workflows with a pipeline overview paragraph and `Figure 1` flowchart. Give exact data sources, resolutions, periods, completeness thresholds, and excluded site counts/reasons; state known biases and bias-correction/normalization steps. Present equations in hierarchical/operational order, test structural assumptions explicitly (such as multivariate normality or tail independence for copulas, or block-size sensitivity), and report sampler/optimizer budgets, convergence checks ($\hat{R} < 1.1$), baseline models, and out-of-sample validation modes (split-sample, leave-one-out, drop-$k$%, or retroactive hindcast).
- **Results:** Group subsections by analytical property or driver isolation (holding one factor fixed while differencing another). In each subsection: orient visual encodings -> report headline pattern -> contrast regions/seasons/models quantitatively -> explain physical/statistical mechanism and diagnose any null results or model artifacts.
- **Discussion, Limitations, Conclusions:** Synthesize mechanisms (complementarity, tail behavior, spatial density) and make concrete modeling/planning recommendations rather than repeating Results numbers. State omitted processes, forcing artifacts, and scale boundaries candidly with how they bound interpretation. Close with 2 to 4 compact Conclusion paragraphs (scope summary, 3 to 4 durable takeaways, bounded extensions) and archive links/DOIs for data and code.

## 3. Target prose patterns (synthetic examples)

- **Literature gap pivot:**
  - _Avoid:_ `Smith (2019) studied floods. Lee (2023) used nonstationary GEV. Nobody has built a revolutionary unified framework!`
  - _Prefer:_ `Regional frequency analysis reduces parameter uncertainty by pooling gauges, while copulas capture pairwise variable dependence. Despite this body of work, existing formulations typically treat spatial pooling, multivariate dependence, and climate nonstationarity in isolation or rely on maximum likelihood fits that struggle on short records. Here we combine Generalized Extreme Value marginals, climate covariates, and a Gaussian elliptical copula in a Bayesian hierarchical framework that propagates parameter uncertainty directly into return levels.`
- **Equation with intuition:**
  - _Prefer:_ `Pairwise dependence between sites is modeled as an exponential function of distance, $R(i, j) = \exp(-\|s_i - s_j\| / \alpha_0)$, where $s_i$ and $s_j$ denote site coordinates and $\alpha_0 > 0$ is the range parameter. Large values of $\alpha_0$ indicate strong coherence across distant gauges, whereas values near zero recover site-by-site independence.`
- **Figure orientation + regional mechanism:**
  - _Prefer:_ `Figure 2 shows compound deficit months from 1982 to 2019 across all 18 control regions ordered from west (bottom) to east (top), where each unit bar height represents one month. Events concentrate in fall and winter across western regions, whereas eastern regions experience far fewer events overall, reflecting stronger snowmelt-driven hydro seasonality and weaker winter wind-solar complementarity in the west.`
- **Candid null result diagnosis:**
  - _Prefer:_ `No statistically significant trend in event duration was detected across infrastructure or climate scenarios (Figure 5). This null result likely stems from the thermodynamic replay forcing, which applies warming perturbations while preserving historical synoptic event sequencing; projected duration changes from this forcing should therefore be interpreted with caution.`
